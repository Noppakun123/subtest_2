# English Quick Guide: Recycling and Waste Sorting in Thailand

## Why sort waste at the source?
Mixed waste lowers the value of recyclables and contaminates them. Paper soaked in food liquid cannot be sold, and plastic bottles covered in leftovers must be washed at extra cost. Sorting at home keeps recyclables clean, keeps hazardous waste out of landfills, and reduces the amount of waste that local authorities must collect and dispose of.

## The four bin colours used in Thailand
- **Green bin – Organic waste**: food scraps, fruit peels, vegetable trimmings, leaves, coffee grounds. Can be composted.
- **Yellow bin – Recyclables**: PET and HDPE bottles, glass bottles, aluminium and steel cans, paper, cardboard, UHT beverage cartons. Must be clean and dry.
- **Blue bin – General waste**: multilayer snack bags, instant noodle sachets, dirty foam boxes, used tissues, diapers. Goes to landfill or incineration.
- **Red bin – Hazardous waste**: fluorescent lamps, batteries, spray cans, pesticide containers, chemical bottles, expired medicines.

## Batteries and lithium-ion safety
Lithium-ion batteries from phones, power banks, e-cigarettes and laptops can catch fire if crushed in garbage trucks. Tape over the terminals, never throw them into general waste, and drop them at a battery collection box or a hazardous waste point. A swollen battery should not be punctured or charged; store it in a non-flammable container away from heat until you can drop it off.

## Electronics (e-waste)
Before disposing of a phone or laptop: back up your data, sign out of all cloud accounts, disable "Find My" features, perform a factory reset, and remove SIM and memory cards. Bring the device to a manufacturer take-back program, a mobile operator's e-waste box, or a municipal hazardous waste point. Avoid informal recyclers that burn cables or use acid to extract metals.

## Composting basics
Balance "greens" (nitrogen-rich food scraps) and "browns" (carbon-rich dry leaves, shredded cardboard) at roughly 2–3 parts brown to 1 part green by volume. Keep the pile as moist as a wrung-out sponge and turn it once or twice a week. Finished compost is dark, crumbly and smells like soil, usually after 2–3 months. Avoid meat, dairy, oil and pet faeces in an open home compost bin.

## "When in doubt, throw it out"
If you are unsure whether an item is recyclable, put it in general waste rather than the recycling bin. One contaminated item can spoil an entire batch of recyclables. This is often called "wishcycling" – putting non-recyclables into the recycling bin in the hope they will be recycled.

## What this guide does not cover
This guide does not list current scrap prices, the opening hours of specific scrap shops, or the exact collection days of a particular municipality. Those details vary by location and over time; please contact your local authority or recycling shop directly.
